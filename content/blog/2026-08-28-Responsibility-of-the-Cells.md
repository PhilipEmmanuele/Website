&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;
Do you ever feel the responsibility of your cells? Trillions of cells. Each of them incredibly complex. All of them working in unison to make you work. To heal you. To provide energy for you. To fight for you. To sacrifice themselves for the good of the whole. You are that whole. 

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;
Your heart flutters as your work crush walks past your desk. All the cells in your heart could beat on their own, but instead they band together. Billions of cells waiting for each other’s cues to fire in synchrony, sixty to eighty to a hundred twenty times every minute. A hundred thousand times, yesterday, today, tomorrow, every day of your life until you die. “Maybe tomorrow,” you think, as your crush turns the corner. 

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;
When you drink, a little part of you dies. Code red ensues on the cellular level. Your hepatocytes fight like hell to break down the ethanol in your blood. Some of them will die, and that is a sacrifice you are willing to make. You take another swig and laugh with your friends.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;
A white blood cell recognizes a stranger. Something that doesn’t belong. It plays a game of rapid and perfect telephone, phoning the cell whose whole life has revolved around this moment. Who is living only to recognize and kill this specific stranger. This cell comes and helps kill off the foreigner. You blink twice and yawn, never knowing there was a threat.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;
When you tan without sunscreen, some of your cells’ DNA mutates. DNA repair proteins rush to correct this before cell replication. “I’m sorry,” the repair proteins say, “but it’s terminal.” They deliver the hard news that the cell is past the point of repair and must sacrifice itself. It cannot be allowed to live. The broken cell takes in its last bit of light before dividing itself into thousands of pieces for its neighbors to eat, careful to spill nothing, delivering itself to cellular heaven. You flip over to tan your back.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;
You finish typing an email to your boss and let out a sigh. On your inhale, a red blood cell is oxygenated and is rushed to the opposite side of your body in less than a minute. It folds itself in half to squeeze through your smallest capillaries and give oxygen to your tissues. Those tissues are starved for oxygen, not able to make the energy you need to live until they receive oxygen from the red blood cell. This red blood cell then turns around and does this again and again, a quarter of a million times until it finally is worn out and dies. Your boss responded already. Another sigh. 

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;
When you are playing football and you take a hit, your brain rattles in your skull. The trauma of the impact disrupts a neuron’s cell membrane, spilling glutamate into the space around it like a scream into a crowded room. Nearby neurons hear the scream and extracellular calcium floods in, overwhelming the mitochondria. Without energy, the cells take their last dying gasps and go dark. The neuron that fine-tuned its call and response to the millions of neurons around it in a chorus to control your precise movements, thoughts, feelings, and bodily functions is permanently removed from the network, never to light up again. You prepare for the next play and the chorus continues, missing voices it’ll never get back. 

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;
As you live, feel the responsibility of your cells. Feel their devotion and the sacrifices that they make, so that you can read this, so that you can live your life every day. Take care of your cells so they can take care of you. And then go do great things. Prove them right in their struggle to help you thrive.
